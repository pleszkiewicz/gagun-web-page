# Ełk traffic snapshot

The application is buildless HTML/CSS/JavaScript. `elk-data.js` is a lazy-loaded local snapshot; no map tiles, API keys, external simulation service or npm packages are needed.

- MZK schedules, stops and shape geometry: [Mikołaj Kuranowski's Ełk GTFS](https://mkuran.pl/gtfs/elk.zip), feed version 2026-11-11, published under **CC0 1.0** on the [feed catalog](https://mkuran.pl/gtfs/). This independently published conversion should be checked against the [operator timetable](https://rozklady.mzk.elk.pl/) when refreshing. - Center: longitude 22.3615813, latitude 53.8248304. The dotted study circle has a 5,000 m radius. Network context extends beyond it to preserve connections.

## Population and fleet baseline

The saved **GUS BDL 2025** observations are stocks as of **31 December 2025**, retrieved 11 November 2026. Use the city of Ełk, not the surrounding rural gmina of the same name.

| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| Residents | 58,889 | City, BDL `042815505011` | GUS observation, variable `72305` |
| Residents used for the fleet ratio | 87,671 | County, BDL `042815505000` | GUS observation, variable `72305` |
| Registered passenger cars | 41,765 | County, BDL `042815505000` | GUS observation, variable `32561` |
| Initial city passenger-car fleet | ≈28,054 | City scenario | Derived estimate |

Sources: [city population](https://bdl.stat.gov.pl/api/v1/data/by-unit/042815505011?var-id=72305&year=2025&format=json), [county population](https://bdl.stat.gov.pl/api/v1/data/by-unit/042815505000?var-id=72305&year=2025&format=json), [county passenger cars](https://bdl.stat.gov.pl/api/v1/data/by-unit/042815505000?var-id=32561&year=2025&format=json). Vehicle data are available at county level. The city estimate assumes the same cars/resident ratio as the county: `round(58889 × 41765 / 87671) = 28054`. Use exact observations rather than the rounded published 476.4 cars/1,000 people. Registered fleet includes cars that may be parked or used elsewhere; it is not a count of vehicles currently moving, nor a measured household ownership rate.

The [county administrative report for 2025](https://bip-v1-files.idcom-jst.pl/sites/47014/wiadomosci/881458/files/raport_o_stanie_powiatu_za_2025_rok.pdf), printed page 69, reports 42,292 passenger cars. It does not explain the difference from GUS's 41,765. This baseline consistently uses GUS population and vehicle observations from the same year, retaining their original geographic scope.

`gus-2025.json` retains the official API responses, including observation attributes and update dates. `city-data.js` is the small generated runtime snapshot. Neither is fetched remotely by the page. To regenerate from the frozen responses, run `python3 scripts/build-elk-city-data.py`; to explicitly refresh the same 2025 series first, add `--refresh`. The generator rejects wrong geographic units, missing/qualified values and invalid counts.

Population/fleet anchor the model's scale and scenario controls. Daily trip frequency, mode choice, spatial origins/destinations, peak shapes and weather response are still assumptions. Population and registration statistics alone do not validate traffic volumes, congestion or journey times against local measurements.

## Data format

Snapshot format version 4 uses a local metric projection centered on Ełk (x east, y south). `nodes` are `[x, y, signal]`. Each edge is `[from, to, nameIndex, kind, vehicleDirectionMask, pedestrianAccess, speedMps, flatGeometry, lanes, turnLanes, osmWayId, cycling]`. Direction masks: 1 forward, 2 reverse, 3 both. Classes: 0 main, 1 tertiary, 2 local, 3 service, 4 pedestrian. Geometry simplification preserves shared intersection nodes. `turns` contains prohibited incoming/outgoing directed arc pairs; an arc ID is `edgeId * 2 + reverse`.

`lanes` is `[forwardCount, backwardCount, sourceFlags]`. Flags: 1/2 explicit or derivable forward/backward counts, 4 mapped total, 8 conflicting tags or ambiguous odd total, 16 a shared single-track road. `turnLanes` contains forward/backward arrays numbered from the right in the travel direction; masks are left 1, through 2, right 4, reverse 8, unrestricted/unknown 15. Center turning lanes (`lanes:both_ways`) do not count as through capacity. An even mapped total alone gets an assumed equal directional split. Missing tags default to one lane per permitted direction. Contradictions retain the legal one-way restriction and are marked uncertain. See [OSM lane semantics](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Key:lanes).

Mapped lane totals cover 117,240 of 555,841 m (21.1%) of drivable edge geometry, across 585 of 3,671 OSM ways. This includes service roads and network context beyond the study circle; it is not a percentage of Ełk's main streets. The selected-road panel identifies the source/assumption and links to the OSM way.

Each transit shape point is `[x, y, GTFSdistanceMeters, roadArcId, roadPositionMeters]`. Shape distance is taken from GTFS, including its kilometer-to-meter conversion. Stops are `[name, x, y]`; trips are `[sourceTripId, shapeIndex, stops, lineId]`, with each stop `[stopIndex, arrivalSeconds, departureSeconds, shapeDistanceMeters]`. `dates` maps an actual service date to active trip indices. Every line variant and calendar is preserved. Shape points with no matching saved road use arc ID `-1`; all 19,482 source shape points inside the 5 km study circle have legal road matches. Unmapped rural sections outside the study circle follow GTFS without car interactions.

`transit.lines` contains `{id, number, name, color, shapes, stops}` records. Shape/stop lists are indices into the shared transit arrays. Colors are distinct application display colors, not operator branding. Each scheduled bus carries its real line ID. The sidebar defaults to line 12, with checkboxes and Show all / Hide all controls. Visibility filters routes, stops, markers, bus details and departures. All lines continue running and influencing traffic/passenger queues when hidden. The bus counter shows selected-line buses, with the selected/all-running counts in its caption; passenger totals always cover the network. Displayed route geometry is clipped to the saved map context; full GTFS geometry remains available to the simulation.

Traffic scripts/styles and the lazy snapshot URL carry cache-version queries. Keep these revisions in sync when changing the schema or UI; the deployment route extractor reads script filenames without their query strings.

## Regenerate

Use Python 3's standard library:

```sh
python3 scripts/build-elk-data.py --osm-dir /path/to/osm-extracts --gtfs /path/to/elk.zip
```

Provide OSM XML map extracts covering at least 6.5 km around the center, with complete lake and city boundary member ways. The script merges duplicate objects by OSM ID and outputs `assets/traffic/elk-data.js`. OSM's map endpoint limits individual extracts to 50,000 nodes; split dense extracts when needed. Production uses only the generated snapshot.

## Demand and weather

The population and passenger-car sliders start from the 2025 baseline and can be changed independently. The travel-assumptions disclosure contains a trips/resident/day slider, default **2** (assumed). Weekday daily demand is `population × tripsPerResident`: 117,778 trips at the baseline. The hourly curve is normalized over a full weekday, so rush hours redistribute this budget rather than add extra trips. With the daily cycle off, demand is flat over 24 hours. Weekends/holidays use a lower travel budget. The curve retains smooth weekday peaks centered at 07:30 and 16:00. Holidays are explicitly listed for the saved date range (11 November and 24–26 December).

Let `R = (fleet / population) / (28054 / 58889)`. Dry car share is `min(0.85, 0.55 × R)`; the remainder is divided 22:15:6:2 between walking, bus, bicycle and scooter travel. This is an aggregate car-availability assumption, not a measured modal split. At baseline it gives 55% car, 22% walking, 15% bus, 6% bicycle and 2% scooter. These shares are assumptions, including cycling/scooter use; they are not measured Ełk modal shares. Increasing residents with a fixed fleet raises total travel and non-car travel; growing residents and fleet together preserves mode shares. A zero population generates no new trips; a zero fleet generates no new car trips.

Full rain displaces 75% of dry-weather walkers, 80% of cyclists and 90% of scooter users. The fraction of those switching to cars is `0.55 × min(1, R)`; the rest switch to bus travel. At the baseline the resulting car/walk/bus/bike/scooter shares are 67.705%, 5.5%, 25.395%, 1.2% and 0.2%. Integer rates and displayed percentages use largest-remainder allocation so the budget stays exact, even for very small demand. Low fleet availability constrains this switch; no cars are invented when the fleet is zero. The total trip budget stays constant. Outside scheduled network availability, bus share is redistributed proportionally among the available car/walking/bike/scooter choices.

The mode bar and trips/day readout describe **city-wide demand**. All 16 saved MZK lines are physically simulated. Passengers choose actual origin/destination stop pairs served by an upcoming trip, within the 5 km study circle, and can board any compatible line. This replaces a per-line ridership allocation assumption. Rural route portions retain the real timetable, but external rural passenger demand and transfers are not modelled. If no city stop pair has service in the next hour, bus share is redistributed to the other four choices. Bus departures remain fixed by the selected GTFS service date for every city/weather/visibility setting.

Rates create travellers continuously. Changing city parameters/rain leaves ongoing trips, queues, bus departures, time and closures intact. The baseline button restores the exact population, estimated fleet and default trip frequency. Lowering fleet prevents new entries above the chosen stock while existing trips finish. Resetting time/date instead seeds an illustrative initial cohort using 8 minutes of car demand, 18 minutes of walking demand, 12 minutes of bicycle demand, 10 minutes of scooter demand, 10 minutes of waiting bus demand and 20 minutes of in-vehicle bus demand shared among active buses. Trip completions retire travellers. Caps of 1,600 cars (also limited by fleet), 2,000 pedestrians, 600 bicycles and 400 scooters bound browser work; unstarted demand remains in a visible backlog rather than being silently discarded. Counters show active simulated car/pedestrian agents and selected/all running buses, not the registered city fleet.

At full rain, car/bus speed limits are reduced by 20%, walking/pushing speed by 10%, bicycle speed by 25%, scooter speed by 35%, and desired car following time increases from 1.3 to 2 seconds. Boarding dwell is 3 seconds plus 1.2–1.5 seconds per boarding passenger and 0.7 seconds per alighting passenger. All coefficients, the mode shares, and bus capacity (80) are assumptions.

Bus demand enters aggregate origin/destination queues at actual stops with an upcoming scheduled service. A bus boards only destinations served later in its actual variant, up to capacity; remaining people keep waiting. Alighting and boarding can add delay to the schedule. Amber stop halos and counts appear when zooming in. The sidebar displays waiting/onboard totals and each bus's load. This is a line-level passenger model, without walking access legs, transfers or full person-level daily activity schedules.

## Cycling network and road closures

Each edge's `cycling` record is `[bikeRideMask, scooterRideMask, facilityForward, facilityBackward, bikePushMask, scooterPushMask]`. Direction masks retain the 1/2/3 convention. Facility types are 0 (none/shared carriageway), 1 (painted lane), 2 (dedicated/shared cycle track). Facilities and access are independent: an access-restricted facility is still mapped as physical infrastructure. The builder retains bicycle-only ways with `foot=no`, bike contraflow and mode-specific directions/access. Crossings connect only where OSM shares a node. Explicit access restrictions and locked barriers affect adjacent split sections. Uninterpreted conditional restrictions are treated conservatively.

The 5 km snapshot contains 61,978 m of mapped tracks/shared paths and 424 m of painted lanes, counted once per edge geometry clipped to the study circle, without doubling directions. Roadside facilities tagged on a street use that street's geometry; they do not invent a separately surveyed centerline. The optional map layer draws separate paths solid and street-tagged facilities dashed. Bicycle/scooter markers and counters remain visible independently of that layer. A marker with a white center represents pushing.

Routing prefers actual cycling infrastructure, with slower walking links where riding is unavailable. Cycling on ordinary allowed carriageways shares the rightmost traffic queue; painted lanes/paths have separate rider queues. Pushing uses 1.25 m/s before weather effects. Ordinary unclassified off-road bicycle paths use a conservative lower speed. Scooters are limited to 20 km/h and ride cycle facilities or carriageways with a confirmed limit no higher than 30 km/h (including living streets). An assumed low car speed is not evidence of a legal scooter route. Plain footways and rough scooter segments are walked. Surface tags, widths, barriers and bicycle-specific junction details remain incomplete; this is an aggregate traffic model, not a navigation/legal-compliance service. See [OSM cycleway tagging](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Key:cycleway) and the [Polish transport ministry's scooter rules](https://www.gov.pl/web/infrastruktura/nowe-przepisy-dotyczace-hulajnog-elektrycznych-i-urzadzen-transportu-osobistego2).

The Manual road closures panel searches named streets and toggles all drivable sections with that name in the saved map. Mixed checkboxes represent partial manual closures created on the map. Active entries show manually closed section count and closed length; unnamed map-selected sections remain individually manageable. Find focuses the relevant road. Clear manual closures removes only manual blocks. `setClosures(ids, closed)` applies the full manual set before routing travellers once; `setClosure` delegates to the same operation. Effective closures are the union of manual blocks and active timed events. Closures do not reset time, demand or ongoing positions. They block carriageways; riders detour or push where foot access permits, while separate cycle tracks remain open. Existing occupants can clear their current section.

## Random accidents and roadworks

`road-events.js` is a browser-local scheduler with a separate reproducible random seed. Accidents and roadworks have independent exponentially distributed intervals (a constant expected rate per simulated hour), with fixed durations taken from their sliders when an event starts. Defaults are **2 accidents/hour lasting 20 minutes** and **0.5 roadworks/hour lasting 90 minutes**. Accident controls allow 0–12/hour and 1–120 minutes; roadwork controls allow 0–4/hour and 5–360 minutes. These are illustrative scenario controls, not Ełk incident statistics or a forecast. Weather, traffic demand and population do not alter the selected event frequency.

Eligible locations are drivable main/tertiary/local road sections at least 15 m long whose geometry midpoint lies within the 5 km study circle. Service roads and pedestrian/cycle-only ways are excluded. Open sections are sampled in proportion to their lengths; this is a spatial assumption, not a measured crash-risk model. A timed event blocks one mapped section in both of its permitted driving directions. Already blocked sections are skipped. If no eligible open section remains, that scheduled arrival is skipped without inventing a closure. This stage models the traffic effect of a closure, not physical collisions or rescue operations. Roadworks are temporary scenario events rather than a live works calendar.

All timers use simulation time: speed controls accelerate them and Pause freezes them. Zero frequency stops new arrivals but lets active events finish. Changing frequency resamples the next arrival of that type from the current time; changing duration affects future events only. The panel shows each road name, planned end time, remaining minutes/seconds, Find and End now. Red exclamation markers distinguish accidents and amber double-bar markers distinguish works. Cars reroute; buses retain their scheduled route and wait before entering the blocked section. On expiry or early completion, the timed block is removed and traffic can resume unless a manual block still covers that section. Clearing a manual block cannot cancel a timed event. The closure version invalidates the map automatically on both opening and closing.

Changing date or time starts a fresh event schedule from that clock and clears active timed events, while preserving manual blocks and slider settings. Restart clears all blocks and resets event counts at 07:30, retaining slider settings. The existing one-day simulation stops at 23:59:59; an event scheduled to end the next day remains active while the clock is stopped, with its next-day end marked in the panel.

## Pilot assumptions

- Seeded, illustrative origins/destinations. Population/fleet controls describe scenario stocks; trips/resident/day describes travel behavior. Parking, external commuter demand, household assignments and a spatially calibrated activity model are not implemented.
- Cars respect mapped one-way/access rules, node-via OSM turn restrictions and available lane-turn markings. Lane counts determine parallel physical queues, road widths and entry choices. Lane changes use a simplified gap check; shared single-track roads hold opposing entries. Buses use the rightmost lane. Exact lane-to-lane connections, dedicated lane restrictions, general overtaking, signal plans, via-way restrictions and detailed pedestrian/vehicle crossing interaction are outside this pilot. Signals use assumed 60-second cycles.
- Pedestrians follow connected walkable ways, including inferred access alongside ordinary streets. Vehicle closures retain pedestrian access.
- Buses follow saved GTFS geometry and service dates. Intermediate stops reserve five seconds before published departure where the interval allows, plus boarding/alighting dwell. Physical queues, rain and closures can propagate delay. No automatic bus detour, depot movements or vehicle rotations yet.
- An occupied closed section is allowed to clear. New vehicle entries stop; cars seek legal alternative routes. If no route is available, they wait. Reopening allows movement again. Clock/date changes reset demand/delays and discard timed events, while preserving manual closures. Restart reopens all sections.
- The snapshot is a historical scenario, not live traffic or a forecast. No claim of calibrated traffic volumes or journey times is made.

## Checks

Run `node --test tests/traffic-simulator.test.cjs` and `python3 -B -m unittest discover -s tests -p 'test_*.py'`. Both use standard libraries only; neither runtime is required by the static page. Checks cover frozen GUS observations/geographic scope, population/fleet effects, daily-budget normalization, empty/fleet-free scenarios, all-line calendars and filtered departure queries, real calendars/geometry, closures and legal turns, rain/peak demand conservation, continuous departures, passenger accounting/capacity, parallel lanes, cycling directions/access, ride/push routing, scooter speed bounds, multi-street closures, OSM lane/cycle-tag parsing, automatic timer expiry/bus release, manual/timed closure ownership, event controls, reproducible event schedules and study-area location limits.
